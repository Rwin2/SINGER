\section{Architecture --- the policy $\pi_\theta$}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The visuomotor policy is a multi-branch network that fuses
\textbf{visual}, \textbf{proprioceptive}, \textbf{historical} and (V9)
\textbf{centroid} features into a single 4-D rate command.

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{align}
\phi^k     &= \mathrm{FE\text{-}MLP}\bigl(\,\mathrm{SqueezeNet}(\mathrm{CLIPSeg}(I^k))\,\bigr)
              & \text{(visual)} \\[2pt]
c^k        &= \Phi_{\text{centroid}}(\mathrm{CLIPSeg}(I^k))
              & \text{(\textcolor{orange}{V9 NEW})} \\[2pt]
h^k        &= \mathrm{HistoryMLP}\bigl(\delta t^{k-5:k-1},\,\delta p_{\W}^{k-5:k-1},\,\delta v_{\W}^{k-5:k-1},\,\delta q_{\Bw}^{k-5:k-1},\,u^{k-5:k-1}\bigr)
              & \text{(temporal)} \\[2pt]
o^k        &= \bigl[\,\phi^k\,\|\,c^k\,\|\,v_{\W}^k\,\|\,q_{\Bw}^k\,\|\,p_{z\W}^k\,\|\,h^k\,\bigr]
              & \text{(concat)} \\[2pt]
\hat{u}^k  &= \pi_\theta(o^k) \;=\; \mathrm{ActionHead}_{[100,100]}(o^k)
              & \text{(action)}
\end{align}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
The CLIPSeg backbone is \emph{frozen} (zero-shot, prompt $=$ object name).
Only the History MLP, Feature Extractor MLP and Action Head are learned.
The centroid extractor $\Phi_{\text{centroid}}$ is purely analytical (no parameters).

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Centroid features $c^k$ --- the V9 addition}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Let $\bm{H} \in [0,1]^{H \times W}$ be the CLIPSeg sigmoid heatmap for the
text prompt (\textit{e.g.} ``green clock''). Define the bright-pixel set
\[
M = \bigl\{\,(x,y)\;\bigm|\;\bm{H}(x,y) > P_{75}(\bm{H})\,\bigr\}.
\]

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{align}
c_x &= \frac{\sum_{(x,y)\in M} x\,\bm{H}(x,y)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in M} \bm{H}(x,y)},
&
c_y &= \frac{\sum_{(x,y)\in M} y\,\bm{H}(x,y)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in M} \bm{H}(x,y)} \\[6pt]
\text{bearing}      &= 2\,c_x / W - 1, &
\text{elevation}    &= 2\,c_y / H - 1, &
\text{app.\ size}\;&= |M| / (H\cdot W) \\[4pt]
c^k &= \bigl[\,\text{bearing},\,\text{elevation},\,\text{app.\ size}\,\bigr]^\top
\end{align}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
\textbf{Intuition.} CLIPSeg gives a $352{\times}352$ heatmap; instead of feeding
that giant tensor to the policy, we collapse it into 3 scalars that encode
\emph{where} the target is (bearing, elevation) and \emph{how big} it appears
(distance proxy). They are concatenated into the state vector --- no new
trainable parameters.

\paragraph{Known V9 caveat.} The same $P_{75}$ threshold is used for both the
centroid mask and the size mask, so $|M|/(HW)\approx 0.25$ is structurally
constant. The +44\,pp BC lift is therefore driven by \textbf{bearing $+$
elevation} alone. V10 fixes this with a fixed sigmoid threshold for size.

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Stage 1 --- Behavioural Cloning (BC)}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Data collection}

A sampling-based motion planner (RRT$^{\!*}$ with semantic goal sets) generates
expert trajectories from random start poses to each target object in the
flightroom Gaussian-Splat scene.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Note} \\
\midrule
trajectory length     & $6$\,s            & at $20$\,Hz $\to 120$ steps \\
trajectories / object & $110$             & 3 objects: clock, leafblower, drill-on-boxes \\
augmentations / traj  & $4$               & domain-random visuals \\
total samples         & $\mathbf{158{,}400}$ & $= 110 \times 3 \times 4 \times 120$ \\
control rate          & $20$\,Hz          & $\Delta t = 50$\,ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Loss --- pure MSE on the action}

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\cL_{\text{BC}}(\theta)
   \;=\; \E_{(o^k,\,u^k_\star)\,\sim\,\D_{\text{BC}}}
         \!\Bigl[\,\bigl\|\pi_\theta(o^k) - u^k_\star\bigr\|_2^{\,2}\,\Bigr]
\;}
\label{eq:bc}
\end{equation}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
where $u^k_\star$ is the expert (RRT-MPC) action at step $k$.
The loss is implemented as
\texttt{nn.MSELoss(reduction='mean')} in
\texttt{src/sousvide/instruct/train\_policy.py}, optimised with Adam at
$\eta = 10^{-4}$, batch size $64$, for $N_{\text{ep}}^{\text{com}} = 150$
epochs on the Commander head and $N_{\text{ep}}^{\text{his}} = 100$ on the
History MLP. Best-checkpoint selection is done on a held-out validation
split (every 5 epochs).

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Stage 2 --- DAgger V9}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DAgger (Dataset Aggregation, Ross et al., 2011) closes the BC distribution
gap by rolling out a \emph{mixed} expert/learner policy in the simulator,
then retraining the learner on the actions the expert would have taken at
the states the learner actually visited.

\subsection{Mixed-policy rollout}

At each step $k$, both the MPC expert and the BC pilot are queried:

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{align}
u^k_{\text{exp}}   &= \mathrm{MPC}(x^k,\,o^k)               & \text{(expert command)}\\
u^k_{\text{pil}}   &= \pi_\theta(o^k)                       & \text{(pilot command)}\\
u^k_{\text{exec}}  &=
   \begin{cases}
     u^k_{\text{exp}} & \text{w.p. } \beta_t \\
     u^k_{\text{pil}} & \text{w.p. } 1-\beta_t
   \end{cases}
   & \text{(executed in sim)}\\[4pt]
\D_{\text{DAg}}    &\leftarrow \D_{\text{DAg}} \cup
   \bigl\{(o^k,\,u^k_{\text{exp}})\bigr\}_{k}    & \text{(annotation)}
\end{align}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
Crucially, the \emph{annotation label is always $u^k_{\text{exp}}$}, even
when the executed action was the pilot's. This is what gives DAgger its
no-regret guarantee.
Implementation: \texttt{class MixedPolicy} in \texttt{train\_dagger.py:951}.

\subsection{Expert-mixing schedule}

$\beta$ decays geometrically across iterations so the rollouts become
progressively pilot-driven:

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{equation}
\beta_t \;=\; \beta_{\text{start}}\cdot\beta_{\text{decay}}^{\,t}
\quad\text{with}\quad
\beta_{\text{start}} = 0.80,\;
\beta_{\text{decay}} = 0.92
\end{equation}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
For $T = 12$ iterations: $\beta_0 = 0.80,\,\beta_1 = 0.736,\,\dots,\,\beta_{11} \approx 0.32$.

\subsection{Annotation aggregation}

V9 uses \textbf{cumulative} aggregation
(\texttt{aggregate\_dagger: true}):
\begin{equation}
\D_{\text{DAg}}^{(t)} \;=\; \D_{\text{DAg}}^{(t-1)} \,\cup\, \D_{\text{new}}^{(t)}
\quad\text{(classic DAgger)}
\end{equation}
Annotations are also \textbf{oversampled} ($\times 20$ duplication,
\texttt{dagger\_oversample: 20}) before being mixed with the BC dataset to
counteract their numerical minority.

\subsection{Retraining loss --- BC, restricted to two heads}

After each iteration the policy is fine-tuned on the (BC $\cup$ DAgger)
mixture. The objective is identical in form to Eq.~\eqref{eq:bc}, but the
\textbf{VisionMLP is frozen} (only the CommanderSV head and the small MLP
on top of the centroid + state stack are updated):

\begin{formulabox}
\begin{align}
\theta &= \theta_{\text{CommanderSV}}
       \quad\text{(VisionMLP frozen --- prevents catastrophic forgetting)}\\[2pt]
\cL_{\text{DAg}}(\theta)
   &= \E_{(o^k,\,u^k_{\text{exp}})\,\sim\,\D_{\text{BC}}\,\cup\,\D_{\text{DAg}}}
      \!\Bigl[\,\bigl\|\pi_\theta(o^k) - u^k_{\text{exp}}\bigr\|_2^{\,2}\,\Bigr]
\end{align}
\end{formulabox}

\noindent
Adam at $\eta_{\text{DAg}} = 2{\times}10^{-5}$ (5$\times$ smaller than BC),
$N_{\text{ep}}^{\text{DAg}} = 10$ epochs per iteration, batch size $64$.
Implementation: \texttt{\_retrain\_commander} in \texttt{train\_dagger.py:2028}.

\subsection{Best-checkpoint reset}

\texttt{reset\_to\_best: true} --- if validation success rate at iteration
$t$ is worse than the best so far, the model weights are restored from the
best checkpoint before iteration $t+1$. Patience $= 5$ iterations before
early stopping. This is what stopped the runaway-regression failure mode
that plagued earlier DAgger versions.

\subsection{Annotation filters}

To prevent garbage-in-garbage-out, an annotation $(o^k, u^k_{\text{exp}})$
is \emph{rejected} if any of the following hold:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Filter} & \textbf{Threshold} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
position deviation         & $> 8$\,m   & runaway pilot, expert query meaningless \\
goal distance              & $> 50$\,m  & lost-in-space trajectories \\
orientation deviation      & $> 90^\circ$ & pilot pointing wrong way \\
close-approach distance    & $< 5$\,m   & near-goal hover regime, low signal \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{One DAgger iteration --- algorithmic summary}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Operation} & \textbf{Notes} \\
\midrule
1 & Query mixed policy in sim ($n_{\text{rol}}{=}8$/object, 3 objects)
  & generates $\approx 3000$ steps \\
2 & Filter annotations (deviation, goal-dist, orient.)
  & drop $\sim$30--50\% of raw \\
3 & Aggregate: $\D_{\text{DAg}} \leftarrow \D_{\text{DAg}} \cup \D_{\text{new}}$
  & cumulative \\
4 & Oversample $\D_{\text{DAg}}$ by $\times 20$
  & balance vs BC data \\
5 & Mix with $\D_{\text{BC}}$ (158\,K)
  & symlink BC observation dirs \\
6 & Retrain Commander only ($\eta=2{\times}10^{-5}$, 10\,ep)
  & VisionMLP frozen \\
7 & Evaluate on $40$ held-out trajectories
  & success / collision / goal-dist \\
8 & If worse than best $\to$ reset to best checkpoint
  & else update best \\
9 & $\beta \leftarrow \beta \cdot 0.92$
  & geometric decay \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Final benchmark --- V9 canonical results}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Pre-Centroid BC} & \textbf{DAgger V7} & \textbf{Centroid BC (V9)} & \textbf{Centroid DAgger (V9)} \\
\midrule
Success           & $36.7\%$ & $52.3\%$ & $80.7\%$ & $\mathbf{88.0\%}$ \\
Collision         & $21.3\%$ & $\sim 20\%$ & $13.3\%$ & $\mathbf{8.0\%}$ \\
Goal dist (m)     & $3.71$ & $\sim 2.5$ & $1.84$ & $\mathbf{1.65}$ \\
Clock             & ---    & $45\%$ & $86\%$ & $\mathbf{90\%}$ \\
Leafblower        & ---    & $60\%$ & $76\%$ & $\mathbf{84\%}$ \\
Drill on boxes    & ---    & $55\%$ & $80\%$ & $\mathbf{90\%}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{Headline lifts.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Centroid features alone: $36.7\% \to 80.7\%$ \quad (\textcolor{orange}{$+44.0$\,pp}, BC vs BC)
  \item DAgger on top of centroid: $80.7\% \to 88.0\%$ \quad (\textcolor{lime}{$+7.3$\,pp})
  \item End-to-end (Pre-Centroid BC $\to$ V9 DAgger): \quad
        $\mathbf{+51.3}$\,pp success, $\mathbf{-13.3}$\,pp collision,
        goal-dist $3.71 \to 1.65$\,m
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Take-away.} Most of the gain comes from the \emph{features},
not from DAgger. Collapsing the CLIPSeg heatmap into 3 scalars gives the BC
policy enough inductive bias that DAgger has comparatively little left to
fix --- but the residual lift it does provide concentrates on the hardest
object (drill-on-boxes, $+10$\,pp) and uniformly reduces collisions across
the board.
